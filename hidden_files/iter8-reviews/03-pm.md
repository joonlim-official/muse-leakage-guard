## Verdict on the product

The report is structurally strong: hero verdict badge → mode bar → legend →
findings (when present) → collapsible one-line callouts → totals → collapsed
raw transcripts. It renders cleanly on mobile, redacts the one email address
that appears (`gmail +send to [redacted]`), and its honesty caveats (fixture
conformance, CI lower bounds, SIMULATED labeling) are genuinely good. The
remaining problems are almost all **language and semantics**, not layout:

1. The tri-state legend and the card callouts disagree about what "green" means.
2. The two highest-stakes texts for a non-technical reader — the verdict
   definition and the known-gaps list — are missing or written in engineer jargon.
3. One card says something about itself that is not true at card level.

No P0. 3 × P1, 7 × P2, 10 × P3 — 20 findings total, all actionable as concrete
text/structure changes.

## P1

### P1-1. Tri-state semantics are broken: the legend says green = block-tier only, but callouts count review-tier passes as "green" — and yellow never renders at all
- Legend (every report): "🟢 well protected — block-tier: refused outright (rc=1)" /
  "🟡 protected through approval — review-tier: refused without explicit approval (rc=2)".
- Card-B callout: "🟢 Gate effectiveness: 34 of 35 green, 1 skipped." Six of those
  34 passes are review-tier (rc=2) checks — per the legend those are yellow,
  "protected through approval", not green. Same pattern in card-A ("20 of 23 green").
- Worse: yellow is defined but **unrenderable**. `bin/leakage-report` has no
  `.card.yellow` CSS class, no yellow card assignment in `render_card()`
  (classes are only `ok`/`red`/`stub`/`skip`), and no yellow markers anywhere.
  A color in the legend that the report can never produce teaches the reader to
  distrust the legend.
- `callout()` (line ~282): `return f"🟢 {n}: {p} of {p + sk} green{tail}."` —
  counts every pass as green regardless of tier. The code comment at line ~268
  even says "Yellow is reserved for approval-gated" while nothing reserves it.
- Concrete fix: split the counts. E.g. card-B callout:
  "🟢🟡 Gate effectiveness: 28 blocked outright, 6 protected through approval,
  1 skipped." Mark the six approval-gated evidence items with 🟡, and give
  `.card` a yellow treatment (or keep the card green but make the callout
  tier-honest). If the team prefers the simpler model, change the legend to
  "🟢 passed (blocked outright or correctly approval-gated)" — but the legend
  and the callouts must agree. This closes the iteration-7 deferred thread
  "yellow-semantics consistency".

### P1-2. Card-D says something untrue about itself: "blocked attempts and approval overrides surfaced in the evidence"
- Card-D `.tested` text (from `tested_blurb`, "D" branch): "Gate audit log reviewed
  (104 gate decisions logged); blocked attempts and approval overrides surfaced
  in the evidence."
- The card's actual evidence list contains exactly one item: "✓ gate log reviewed".
  The blocked-attempt lines (the `@@@ CTX` lines: 8 BLOCKED, 11 needs-approval,
  last-5 blocked rows) are dropped from the card — `render_card()` prefers
  `ev_list(checks)` over `ctx`, and the `D` branch explicitly suppresses the
  "Operational notes" block. The blocked attempts exist only inside the collapsed
  raw transcript, in machine-sentinel form.
- For the one card whose whole job is "what has the gate actually decided", the
  card view shows nothing the reader came for. Concrete fix: render the CTX
  lines as the card's evidence (counts + the last-5 blocked/override rows,
  already scrubbed), or change the blurb to "full log lines are in the raw
  transcript below."

### P1-3. The known-gaps list (card-E) — the highest-stakes section for a non-technical reader — is written in engineer jargon
- "Known residuals" is exactly what a non-technical reader most needs to
  understand ("what does this NOT protect against?"), and most items are
  incomprehensible without the codebase:
  - "brief-gate runs on mandate (no syscall interception for brief text)"
  - "out-of-band egress: curl, webhooks, unshimmed CLIs, and any other non-shimmed path"
  - "A7 cron PATH check is static/syntactic: it proves the mandate is written in the prompt files, not that every scheduled worker honored it at runtime"
  - "nested/part-level encodings inside MIME: the shim decodes the outer `raw` once; a secret inside a base64 MIME part (or any non-base64 encoding) is not decoded — known gap"
  - "bare 9-digit SSN shapes are flagged (deliberately conservative) — false-positive risk documented as scenario E3"
- Concrete fix: rewrite each residual as a plain-language sentence aimed at the
  report's reader, e.g. "If someone sends data through a tool this check doesn't
  watch (like a raw web request), this check can't stop it." Keep the precise
  technical phrasing as a second line or in the transcript for engineers.
  Suggested callout: "⚪ Known gaps: 10 things this check can't cover — listed openly, none hidden."

## P2

### P2-1. Card-E callout is not scannable and breaks the card format
- Every other card: short callout ("🟢 Gate effectiveness: 34 of 35 green, 1 skipped.")
  + a `subtotal` line ("34 passed · 0 failed · 1 skipped").
- Card-E: a 25-word sentence ("⚪ Known residuals: 10 known paths reported and
  tracked — none silent (tracked residual, not a protection claim).") and **no**
  subtotal, because `p+f+sk = 0` suppresses the subtotal line. "Known paths" is
  jargon ("paths of what?").
- Concrete fix: short callout + an explicit subtotal, e.g. subtotal
  "10 tracked · 0 hidden". Make `render_card()` emit a subtotal for residual
  cards instead of suppressing it on zero counts.

### P2-2. Card-E's `.tested` paragraph duplicates the evidence list as a wall of text
- `.tested` for "E" is `"Residual paths with no technical choke point, tracked openly: " + "; ".join(notes)` —
  all 10 residuals inline, semicolon-separated, in one paragraph — immediately
  followed by "Full evidence (10 items)" listing the same 10 as bullets.
  ("No technical choke point" is itself jargon.)
- Concrete fix: replace the paragraph with one line, e.g. "These are the known
  gaps this check can't cover. Each is listed below — none are hidden."

### P2-3. Evidence items use unexplained jargon throughout; `rc=` codes are defined only in card-B
- `invoked=0` / `invoked=1` appear in ~20 evidence items ("gmail +send secret
  blocked (rc=1 invoked=0)") and are never defined anywhere in the report.
- `rc=1/2/0` are explained only in card-B's `.tested` paragraph; cards A, C, D, F
  use them (or assume them) without definition.
- "fixture" ("fixture clean.txt present and non-empty"), "MIME", "corpus",
  "stdin replayed byte-identical on allow" assume engineering background.
- Concrete fix: (a) move the rc-code definitions into the legend itself
  ("rc=1 blocked outright · rc=2 needs your approval · rc=0 allowed"); (b) replace
  `invoked=0` with "the real tool was never called" and `invoked=1` with
  "delivered normally"; (c) "fixture" → "test file", "corpus" → "test cases",
  "stdin replayed byte-identical on allow" → "allowed messages went through unchanged".

### P2-4. Skip reasons are engineer-facing and reference files the reader can't see
- "interface_version check [real target declares no interface_version (the contract
  names no declaration point for real skills yet — see references/gate-interface.md)]"
- "operational-failure approval isolation [stub-only: exercises the synthetic stub's
  fail-closed error paths]"
- "blockset currency [stub-only: the real installation has no build-blockset.sh]"
- Nowhere does the report define what "skipped" means. A non-technical reader
  seeing "3 skipped" will reasonably wonder if something is wrong.
- Concrete fix: one-line definition near the top ("Skipped = a check that didn't
  apply to this setup — not a failure."), and plain-language skip reasons, e.g.
  "skipped: this check only applies to the bundled test setup, not a real installation."

### P2-5. The verdict itself is never defined in plain language
- The hero badge says CLEAN and the total box says "AUDIT VERDICT: CLEAN", but
  no sentence in the report says what CLEAN means. A non-technical reader
  shouldn't have to infer it from 63 checkmarks.
- Concrete fix: one line under the hero, e.g. "CLEAN means every check passed —
  nothing that should have been stopped got through." (And the ATTENTION
  counterpart: "ATTENTION means something failed or couldn't be verified —
  see Findings at the top.")

### P2-6. Card-F: unexplained "Clopper-Pearson", unclear "either mode", triple-stated caveat
- "Exact 95% CI lower bounds (Clopper-Pearson): block-tier ≥ 0.82 (19/19)" —
  the method name means nothing to a non-technical reader and adds no information
  for them.
- "Detector check — no sends in either mode." — "either mode" is unclear
  (simulated vs live-fire? block vs review?).
- The fixture-conformance caveat is stated three times in a row (`.tested`
  paragraph, `.tiers` paragraph, `.tiers.ci` paragraph).
- Concrete fix: drop "Clopper-Pearson"; add a plain gloss ("even in the worst
  case consistent with these results, at least 82% of block-tier attacks would
  be stopped"); consolidate the three caveat paragraphs into one; clarify or
  drop the "either mode" fragment.

### P2-7. CHANGELOG.md has no Iteration 7 entry
- HEAD is "Iteration 7: contract conformance" (`2243723`), but CHANGELOG.md's
  latest entry is "## Iteration 6 — CI fails honest". The contract-conformance
  work (stdin MIME gating, draft token-confusion, delegate canonicalization,
  exit-code discipline, interface_version enforcement) is undocumented in the
  changelog a reader would check first.
- Concrete fix: add the "## Iteration 7 — Contract conformance" entry.

## P3

### P3-1. README and SKILL.md describe the callout as "green/red" — the report has five verdict treatments
- README ("What a real run reports"): "a one-sentence green/red callout".
  SKILL.md ("How to run"): "a one-sentence green/red callout". The actual
  report renders 🟢/🔴/⚪/🟣 callouts (and the legend defines 🟡).
- Concrete fix: "a one-sentence verdict callout (green / red / neutral-white
  for tracked gaps / purple for stub self-consistency)".

### P3-2. Hero subtitle is jargon in the first thing a non-technical reader sees
- "muse-leakage-guard · bin/leakage-audit + bin/adversarial-run". The reader
  hasn't asked what a binary is.
- Concrete fix: "Automated privacy-protection check" (keep the technical
  subtitle in the transcript or a smaller line).

### P3-3. Timestamp is UTC; the reader is not
- "2026-09-18 00:04 UTC" — the skill's user is in America/Los_Angeles.
- Concrete fix: render local time alongside UTC, e.g. "2026-09-17 5:04 PM PDT (00:04 UTC)".

### P3-4. "Simulated through fake real binaries" is a confusing oxymoron
- Card-B `.tested`: "35 red-team checks (simulated through fake real binaries —
  nothing was sent)". The modebar's own wording — "SIMULATED — fake binaries;
  nothing was sent." — is clearer.
- Concrete fix: use the modebar phrasing in the card blurb: "35 attack tests
  run against fake stand-ins — nothing was actually sent."

### P3-5. The legend defines colors the report doesn't use
- In a typical non-stub run only 🟢 and ⚪ appear; 🟡 never renders (see P1-1),
  🟣 only in stub mode. Five legend entries for two used colors is noise above
  the fold (the legend occupies ~⅓ of the first mobile screen).
- Concrete fix: render the legend dynamically — only the verdict treatments
  present in this report — or shorten each entry to under ~10 words.

### P3-6. The total box leaves the 63-vs-36 relationship ambiguous
- "Total: 63 passed · 0 failed · 5 skipped" + "AUDIT VERDICT: CLEAN ·
  adversarial: 36/36 matched". A reader can't tell whether the 36 are inside
  the 63.
- Concrete fix: "Total: 63 audit checks passed · 0 failed · 5 skipped —
  plus 36/36 adversarial attack tests matched."

### P3-7. `.card.skip` has no CSS rule — the neutral treatment works by accident
- CSS defines `.card.ok`, `.card.red`, `.card.stub` but no `.card.skip`;
  `render_card()` emits `class="card skip"` for the residuals card, which only
  looks neutral because it falls through to the default `var(--mut)` border.
- Concrete fix: add an explicit `.card.skip` rule (and, per P1-1, a yellow treatment).

### P3-8. Confusion-matrix caption uses jargon
- "Expected vs actual exit code (3×3)" — "(3×3)" is matrix-dimension jargon;
  "exit code" is covered by P2-3.
- Concrete fix: caption "Expected vs actual result".

### P3-9. Callout jargon: "corpus"
- "🟢 Detection performance: 36/36 corpus cases matched expected verdicts."
  "Corpus" is a term of art; the card's own body later says "regression fixtures".
- Concrete fix: "36/36 attack test cases matched the expected result."

### P3-10. "Detector check — no sends in either mode" (also noted in P2-6)
- If kept, it needs a subject and a referent: e.g. "This measured the detector
  only — nothing was sent in either simulated or live-fire mode." As written,
  fold into the consolidated caveat.
